## Template 1: Supplier Emissions Data Request

Send this to Tier-1 suppliers. Keep it short: response rate drops with every extra field.

| Field | What to ask | Why |
|-------|-------------|-----|
| **Boundary** | "Do you report Scope 1 and 2? Under which standard (GHG Protocol, ISO 14064-1)?" | Tells you the data's basis |
| **Scope 1** | "Total Scope 1 in tCO₂e for your last reporting year." | The core number |
| **Scope 2** | "Scope 2 — both location-based and market-based." | Enables fair comparison |
| **Product intensity** | "Emissions per unit of the product we buy (kg CO₂e per unit/t/€)." | The *useful* number for Cat 1 |
| **Assurance** | "Is your inventory third-party verified? By whom?" | Tells you data quality |
| **Targets** | "Do you have an SBTi or equivalent target? What year?" | Signals trajectory |
| **Data vintage** | "Which calendar year does this cover?" | Avoids stale data |

> **Remember the incentive problem** (see [04 Abatement Challenges](04_abatement_challenges.md)): offer something back — longer contracts, preferential financing, or a clear "this will not be used to delist you" signal.

## Template 3: Data Collection Checklist

Tick each as you go. The order is deliberate — biggest categories first.

- [ ] Identify material categories (Template 2).
- [ ] For each material category, name the **activity data** you need (spend, t·km, units, kWh…).
- [ ] For each, name the **emission factor** you'll use — and its **source, year, and geography**.
- [ ] Collect activity data from internal systems (ERP, travel, logistics, energy bills).
- [ ] Send supplier data requests (Template 1) for the top suppliers by spend.
- [ ] Decide the method per category: primary / hybrid / average-data / spend-based.
- [ ] Build the calculation with **documented assumptions** (esp. Cat 11 lifetime and grid).
- [ ] Run the arithmetic and **sanity-check against peers** (see [07 Data](07_industry_data_and_benchmarks.md)).
- [ ] Re-run with one assumption changed — **note how much the number moves**.
- [ ] Store the source data and methodology so an auditor can reproduce it.

## Template 4: Category Prioritisation Worksheet

A simple 2×2 to decide *where to act*, not just *what to measure*.

| | **High influence** (you control it) | **Low influence** (you don't) |
|---|---|---|
| **Large emissions** | **Act now** — product redesign, supplier switching | **Finance & engage** — co-invest, inset finance |
| **Small emissions** | **Quick wins** — efficiency, procurement specs | **Monitor only** — disclose, don't over-invest |

- *High influence + large* → this is your strategy. Example: an OEM's EV mix on Cat 11.
- *Low influence + large* → this needs a market or financing mechanism, not a questionnaire. Example: Cat 11 grid decarbonisation, or Cat 1 from hard-to-abate suppliers (see [04](04_abatement_challenges.md)).

## Template 5: Disclosure & Assurance Readiness

Before you publish (or before an auditor arrives):

- [ ] **Category breakdown** disclosed, at least for material categories — not just a total.
- [ ] **Methods and assumptions** stated per category (which EF database, which grid scenario, which lifetime).
- [ ] **Base year** defined, and restatement policy documented.
- [ ] **Exclusions** justified — why any category is omitted.
- [ ] **Data sources** traceable: every number can be mapped to a source document.
- [ ] **Third-party assurance** scoped and scheduled (ISO 14064-3 / limited assurance).
- [ ] **Targets** linked to a transition plan with levers, capital, and milestones.
- [ ] **Uncertainty** acknowledged — say what's an estimate and how wide the range is.

> This checklist maps directly to the CSRD/ESRS E1 and ISSB S2 expectations in [06 Standards](06_standards_regulatory.md).

---

## Key Takeaways

- **Start lean** — one piece of activity data and one factor per material category is enough for a first pass.
- **Materiality first**: two categories usually dominate; spend your effort there.
- **Document as you go** — if you can't reproduce it, you can't assure it.
