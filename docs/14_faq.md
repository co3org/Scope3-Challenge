> **In short:** Scope 3 is **70–90% of most companies' footprint**; it is hard to measure because the data lives with suppliers who have little incentive to share it; and it cannot be "solved" by offsets — the credible fix is verified in-value-chain reduction (insetting) with attributable accounting. The single biggest near-term unknown is the **GHG Protocol revision**.

### Why does Scope 3 matter more than Scopes 1 and 2?

Because for most companies, **Scope 3 is 70–90% of the real footprint** — and Scope 1 + 2 combined cover only 10–30%. An automotive OEM's tailpipe emissions, a bank's loan book, an apparel brand's cotton fields and dyeing mills: none appear in Scope 1 or 2. A climate pledge that ignores Scope 3 addresses, at best, a third of the problem. ([01 Fundamentals](01_scope3_fundamentals.md))

---

### Why do so few companies disclose Scope 3 properly?

Only **57.8%** of the 1,391 companies in the Carbon3 registry provide category-level Scope 3 breakdowns, and **25.7%** disclose nothing at all. The reasons are structural rather than lazy: data lives with suppliers who have little incentive to share it, the standards are fragmented, and the cheapest methods (spend-based) produce numbers that look complete without being accurate. ([07 Data](07_industry_data_and_benchmarks.md))

---

### Why is measuring Scope 3 so inaccurate?

Because most companies use **spend-based emission factors** — multiplying spend by an industry average — which can be off by **±100–400%**. The error is directional: high-carbon suppliers in low-cost regions are systematically *undercounted*. Emission-factor databases also disagree by 2–3x for the same process. ([02 Complexity](02_complexity_challenges.md))

---

### Why don't suppliers just share their data?

The **incentive trap**: the buyer is responsible for Scope 3 reporting, but the supplier holds the data — and sharing it exposes the supplier's carbon intensity to competitive risk and raises its cost of disclosure, with no direct financial benefit flowing back. ([02 Complexity](02_complexity_challenges.md))

---

### Why isn't measuring the same as reducing?

Knowing *where* your emissions are does not tell you *how* to reduce them. The measurement stack (GHG Protocol, emission factors, accounting software, verification) is increasingly solved; the abatement stack — who pays for supplier decarbonisation, how reductions are verified and attributed — is mostly absent. ([04 Abatement Challenges](04_abatement_challenges.md))

---

### What's the difference between offsetting and insetting?

- **Offsetting** finances reductions *outside* your value chain (a forest in Brazil, a cookstove in Kenya). It cannot be attributed to a specific Scope 3 category, and its additionality is hard to verify.
- **Insetting** finances reductions *inside* your value chain (your supplier switches fuel, your farmer adopts regenerative practice). It is attributable to a Scope 3 category and strengthens the supplier relationship.

([05 Carbon3 Solution](05_carbon3_solution.md))

---

### Are carbon offsets effectively dead?

Not dead, but structurally restricted. Quality scandals collapsed nature-based offset prices to **$2–8/tCO₂e** in 2023–2024, and SBTi/VCMI now confine offsets to *residual* emissions after all feasible reductions. High-quality industrial credits still trade at **$15–40/tCO₂e**. Offsets are being pushed to the margins; insetting is the direction of travel. ([06 Standards](06_standards_regulatory.md))

---

### What is an inset credit, and how is it verified?

One **tCO₂e of verified reduction within a supply chain**, issued as a tradable asset. Verification follows ISO 14064-2 (quantification against a baseline) and ISO 14064-3 (independent third-party audit), with a full audit trail from project registration to retirement. ([05 Carbon3 Solution](05_carbon3_solution.md))

---

### What is the biggest risk to the inset-credit model?

The **GHG Protocol revision** (concluding 2025–2026). If the revised Protocol allows buyers to claim a supplier's verified Scope 1 reduction in their own Scope 3 accounts, the model is validated. If reductions remain in the supplier's accounts only, buyers cannot directly claim inset credits as their own reduction. Both outcomes are plausible. ([05 Carbon3 Solution](05_carbon3_solution.md), [06 Standards](06_standards_regulatory.md))

---

### Is Scope 3 disclosure actually mandatory anywhere?

Yes, but unevenly. The **EU's CSRD/ESRS E1** is the most comprehensive mandatory regime (all scopes, double materiality, third-party assurance). **California's SB 253** (from 2026) and the **ISSB S2** adoptions add pressure. The **US SEC rule** was finalized in 2024 but is functionally paused. ([06 Standards](06_standards_regulatory.md))

---

### Why do banks' net-zero claims hinge on financed emissions?

Because a large bank's financed emissions (Scope 3 Cat 15) are typically **100–1,000x** its own operational footprint. A net-zero claim that excludes the loan book is, in practical terms, meaningless. PCAF provides the attribution methodology, but it rewards divestment even when engagement produces better real-world outcomes. ([03 Case Studies](03_industry_case_studies.md))

---

### What does "additionality" mean, and why is it so hard?

An emission reduction is **additional** only if it would *not* have happened without the credit financing. That requires counterfactual reasoning — observing the world where the credit didn't exist — which is impossible. Verifiers use proxies (financial analysis, policy scans, barrier assessments), but those can be gamed, and policy tightening (CBAM, EU ETS) is steadily eroding the additionality of many once-additional actions. ([04 Abatement Challenges](04_abatement_challenges.md))

---

### What should a company actually do about Scope 3 today?

A pragmatic sequence, in order of leverage:

1. **Measure material categories first** with primary supplier data — avoid the spend-based trap where the footprint is largest.
2. **Set an engagement target, but pair it with a financing mechanism** — engagement without capital rarely causes reduction.
3. **Target the cheapest abatement** (supplier energy efficiency, renewable procurement) before the expensive.
4. **Watch the GHG Protocol revision** — it will determine how supplier reductions can be claimed.
5. **Build the audit trail now**, so any claimed reduction survives CSRD assurance.

([04 Abatement Challenges](04_abatement_challenges.md), [10 Quick Reference](15_quick_reference.md))

---

### Is Carbon3 just another offset scheme?

No. The distinction is **location and attribution**: Carbon3's inset credits represent reductions *inside* the buyer's value chain, attributable to a specific Scope 3 category, verified to ISO 14064, and traded on a marketplace with an allocation engine. But the honest answer is that the model carries real, disclosed risks — most notably GHG Protocol recognition and additionality erosion. ([05 Carbon3 Solution](05_carbon3_solution.md))

---

## Key Takeaways

- Scope 3's difficulty is **structural**, not technical — the answers above all reduce to data, incentives, and governance.
- **Offsetting is out; insetting is in** — but insetting's accounting treatment is not yet settled.
- The single most important event to watch is the **GHG Protocol revision**.
