## A-C

**Additionality** — The requirement that an emission reduction would *not* have occurred without the financing provided by the credit buyer. It is the make-or-break test for both offsets and insets, and is notoriously hard to prove (see [04 Abatement Challenges](04_abatement_challenges.md)).

**Attribution factor** — Under the [PCAF](#p-s) standard, the share of a borrower's or investee's emissions allocated to a financial institution — typically the ratio of the outstanding loan or investment value to the borrower's enterprise value.

**Avoided emissions** — Emissions that would hypothetically have occurred under a counterfactual scenario. Distinct from *reductions* (measured against a real, audited baseline); avoided-emission claims are weaker because the counterfactual is unobservable.

**Baseline** — The pre-intervention emission intensity used as the reference point for measuring a reduction. A credible baseline is the foundation of any carbon credit.

**Biogenic emissions** — CO₂ emitted or removed by living or recently living biological material (crops, livestock, forests). Treated separately from fossil CO₂ in GHG accounting because the carbon cycle timescales differ.

**BVCM (Beyond Value Chain Mitigation)** — Climate finance directed outside a company's own value chain — the category into which traditional offsets increasingly fall, distinct from in-value-chain insetting.

**Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM)** — The EU's carbon-pricing mechanism on imported carbon-intensive goods, designed to prevent "carbon leakage" as domestic emitters face rising carbon costs.

**Carbon credit** — A transferable instrument representing one tonne of CO₂e reduced or removed. *Offsets* originate outside the buyer's value chain; *insets* originate within it.

**Carbon neutrality** — A claim that emissions are balanced by equivalent reductions or removals. Loose and increasingly regulated; see [06 Standards](06_standards_regulatory.md) for the VCMI claim tiers.

**Category (Scope 3)** — One of the 15 categories in the GHG Protocol Scope 3 Standard: upstream Cat 1–8 and downstream Cat 9–15. See [01 Fundamentals](01_scope3_fundamentals.md).

**CDP** — The Carbon Disclosure Project, the dominant voluntary environmental-disclosure platform (23,000+ companies), now aligned with TCFD/ISSB.

**CSDDD** — The EU Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive, which creates a *legal* (not just reporting) obligation to identify and remediate environmental and human-rights harms in the value chain.

**CSRD / ESRS E1** — The EU Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive and its climate standard, the most comprehensive mandatory Scope 3 disclosure regime currently in effect.

## D-F

**Double counting** — The same physical tonne of emissions appearing in more than one company's accounts. By design in Scope 3 (a company-level risk tool, not a system inventory); problematic when a single reduction is claimed by both a supplier and a buyer.

**Double materiality** — The CSRD requirement to assess both *financial* materiality (how climate affects the company) and *impact* materiality (how the company affects climate). Contrast with the single (financial) materiality of ISSB/SEC.

**Downstream** — Scope 3 Categories 9–15: emissions from what happens *after* a company sells its product — distribution, use, end-of-life, franchises, and investments.

**Emission factor (EF)** — A conversion ratio such as "kg CO₂e per dollar of steel" or "kg CO₂e per tonne-kilometre." Databases disagree by 2–3x for the same process; see [02 Complexity](02_complexity_challenges.md).

**Engagement target** — An SBTi-allowed Scope 3 target type measured as "X% of suppliers have targets" rather than an absolute reduction. Engagement ≠ reduction (see [04 Abatement Challenges](04_abatement_challenges.md)).

**Enterprise value attribution** — The PCAF allocation method that ties financed emissions to financial ownership. Its anomaly: a high market valuation produces *lower* attributed emissions per unit of output than a depressed one — carbon inversely proportional to market cap.

**Financed emissions** — Scope 3 Category 15: the emissions attributable to a financial institution's loans and investments. Typically 100–1,000x the institution's own operational footprint.

**FLAG** — SBTi's Forest, Land and Agriculture guidance, covering biogenic emissions and land-sector targets.

**Fugitive emissions** — Unintended leaks such as refrigerants or methane from pipelines, part of Scope 1.

## G-I

**GHG Protocol** — The World Resources Institute / WBCSD standard that underpins almost all corporate carbon accounting. Voluntary, not regulatory; used by 90%+ of the Fortune 500. Currently under its first major revision since 2011.

**Greenwashing** — Overstating the environmental benefit of an action or claim. The 2023–2024 voluntary-carbon-market scandals were driven largely by weak additionality and permanence in offset projects.

**Hard-to-abate sectors** — Steel, cement, aviation, shipping, chemicals, and agricultural methane — where process emissions or energy density make abatement expensive with current technology.

**ICVCM** — The Integrity Council for the Voluntary Carbon Market, which sets the Core Carbon Principles governing *issuance* quality.

**Inset / inset credit** — A verified emission reduction *within* a company's own value chain, converted into a tradable asset. The core instrument of [05 Carbon3 Solution](05_carbon3_solution.md). Contrast with an offset.

**ISO 14064** — The international standard series: 14064-1 (organizational), 14064-2 (project-level quantification), 14064-3 (verification and validation).

**ISSB S2 (IFRS S2)** — The ISSB's climate-disclosure standard (2023), successor to TCFD, adopted by jurisdictions representing 55%+ of global GDP. Uses single (financial) materiality.

## L-N

**LkSG** — Germany's Supply Chain Due Diligence Act (*Lieferkettensorgfaltspflichtengesetz*), in force since 2023, requiring climate-related supply-chain diligence from large German employers.

**Location-based method** — Scope 2 accounting using the average grid intensity where the electricity is consumed, regardless of contractual choices.

**LUC (Land Use Change)** — Emissions from converting land (especially tropical forest or peatland) to agriculture. Emission-factor choices here can swing a footprint by 15–40x; see [03 Case Studies](03_industry_case_studies.md).

**Market-based method** — Scope 2 accounting reflecting contractual instruments such as RECs and green tariffs, distinct from location-based.

**Materiality** — The test for whether a Scope 3 category must be disclosed. Self-assessed under the GHG Protocol; made rigorous under CSRD's double materiality.

**Net-zero** — Balancing anthropogenic emissions with removals at a specified point (typically 2050). A commitment is meaningless for most companies without a credible Scope 3 plan.

---

## P-S

**PACT** — WBCSD's Pathfinder framework for exchanging *product-level* carbon footprints directly between suppliers and buyers. Solves measurement, not abatement.

**PCAF** — The Partnership for Carbon Accounting Financials, the standard extending the GHG Protocol to financial institutions' financed emissions.

**Primary data** — Emission data obtained directly from a supplier (supplier-specific method), rather than from sector averages or spend factors. The gold standard, and the rarest.

**REC (Renewable Energy Certificate)** — A market instrument certifying one MWh of renewable generation. The accounting analogy for insets is useful but imperfect (see [05 Carbon3 Solution](05_carbon3_solution.md)).

**RFI (Radiative Forcing Index)** — The multiplier for aviation's non-CO₂ warming effects (contrails, NOx). The IPCC estimates 2–4x; the GHG Protocol does not require its use.

**SBTi** — The Science Based Targets initiative, the gold standard for corporate climate target validation. Its Scope 3 targets are mandatory for most companies yet depend on factors they do not control.

**Scope 1 / 2 / 3** — Direct emissions (Scope 1), purchased energy (Scope 2), and value-chain emissions (Scope 3).

**Spend-based method** — Estimating emissions by multiplying spend by a sector-average emission factor. The least accurate method (±100–400%), but the most common because it needs only financial data.

**Supplier engagement** — The dominant Scope 3 "abatement strategy": surveying suppliers, setting engagement targets, and reporting participation — without necessarily causing or measuring real reductions.

## T-W

**TCFD** — The Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures; disbanded in 2023 and folded into ISSB S2.

**TNFD** — The Taskforce on Nature-related Financial Disclosures, extending the TCFD framework to biodiversity and nature, which GHG accounting does not capture.

**Upstream** — Scope 3 Categories 1–8: emissions from what a company *buys* and how it buys it.

**VCM (Voluntary Carbon Market)** — The market for voluntary carbon credits, worth ~$535M in 2024 — orders of magnitude smaller than the implied Scope 3 carbon liability it might one day serve.

**VCMI** — The Voluntary Carbon Markets Integrity Initiative, which governs how companies *claim* credit use (Silver/Gold/Platinum tiers). Neither VCMI nor ICVCM has fully addressed inset credits.

**Verification** — Independent third-party confirmation that a reduction is real, measured against a valid baseline, and additional (ISO 14064-3).

**Vintage** — The year in which an emission reduction occurred, recorded on each credit.

**Well-to-gate / well-to-wheel** — Lifecycle boundaries for fuels: well-to-gate covers extraction through production; well-to-wheel adds combustion in use.

---

## Key Takeaways

- The field's core vocabulary maps onto **three problems**: *measurement* (emission factors, scopes, categories), *credibility* (additionality, verification, double counting), and *market* (offsets vs. insets, VCM/VCMI/ICVCM).
- Most confusion between "carbon neutral," "net-zero," "offset," and "inset" is resolved by **location** (inside vs. outside the value chain) and **rigour** (additionality + verification).
- Several definitions here are **contested or unresolved** — inset accounting, materiality, and aviation's RFI chief among them.
